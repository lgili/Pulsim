    Tutorial 10 — From datasheet curves to a double-pulse test¶

    Goal. Turn the curves of a power-MOSFET datasheet into an add_power_mosfet device with the fitters of pulsim.datasheet, then measure its switching energies with pulsim.double_pulse_test.

    Concepts introduced: - fit_mosfet_channel, fit_rdson_temperature, fit_charge_table, power_mosfet_from_datasheet — each returns its residual and refuses a fit above its limit. - double_pulse_test — the clamped inductive double pulse, energies integrated on the result's own time grid. - Fitted data versus held-out data: a curve the parameters were fitted to cannot validate them.

    The curves below are SYNTHETIC. They are generated from known parameters so that the round trip can be checked; they are not a real part. A real part's curves are digitised from its datasheet (record the document, revision and figure number), and its datasheet switching energies stay out of the fit: they are the held-out check.

    1. Curves¶

    import numpy as np
import pulsim as p
from pulsim import datasheet as ds

# Transfer curve in saturation (V_DS = 20 V): I_D = K (V_GS - V_T)^2.
v_gs = np.linspace(4.5, 10.0, 12)
i_d = 1.43 * (v_gs - 3.0) ** 2

# Capacitances at V_GS = 0 against V_DS, as printed on a datasheet.
v_ds = np.array([0.0, 1.0, 10.0, 100.0, 400.0])
C_iss = np.column_stack([v_ds, [3e-9, 2.6e-9, 2.2e-9, 2.05e-9, 2.02e-9]])
C_oss = np.column_stack([v_ds, [2e-9, 1.4e-9, 5e-10, 1.5e-10, 8e-11]])
C_rss = np.column_stack([v_ds, [1e-9, 6e-10, 2e-10, 5e-11, 2e-11]])

# Normalised R_DS(on) against junction temperature.
T_j = np.array([-40.0, 25.0, 75.0, 125.0, 150.0])
r_norm = 1 + 5e-3 * (T_j - 25) + 2e-5 * (T_j - 25) ** 2

    2. Fit¶

    ch = ds.fit_mosfet_channel(v_gs, i_d, v_ds=20.0)
print(f"K = {ch['K']:.4g} A/V^2, V_T = {ch['V_T']:.4g} V, residual {ch['residual']:.1e}")

out = ds.power_mosfet_from_datasheet(
    transfer={"v_gs": v_gs, "i_d": i_d, "v_ds": 20.0},
    R_ds_on=0.1, V_gs_spec=10.0,
    C_iss=C_iss, C_oss=C_oss, C_rss=C_rss, R_g=2.0,
    rds_temperature={"T_j": T_j, "r_norm": r_norm})
kw = out["kwargs"]
print({k: round(float(v), 9) for k, v in out["residuals"].items()})
assert abs(kw["K"] - 1.43) < 1e-6 and abs(kw["rds_tc1"] - 5e-3) < 1e-9

    C_gd and C_ds come back as charge-table laws (ChargeLaw.table): the capacitor is stamped in charge form, so charge is conserved whatever C(v) does. R_d is what R_DS(on) leaves after the channel's own 1/(2K(V_GS − V_T)); a negative value is refused (the transfer curve and R_DS(on) disagree).

    3. Double pulse¶

    r = p.double_pulse_test(kw, V_dd=400.0, I_load=10.0, R_g_ext=10.0,
                        V_drive=12.0, L_load=500e-6, engine="trbdf2")
print(f"E_on {r.E_on * 1e6:.2f} uJ, E_off {r.E_off * 1e6:.2f} uJ, "
      f"plateau {r.t_plateau * 1e9:.1f} ns at {r.V_plateau:.2f} V")
assert r.E_on > 0 and r.E_off > 0

    Use engine="trbdf2": the gate transient lasts tens of nanoseconds, and the fixed-step pwl engine needs a step that resolves it (dt=2e-10 for a 60 ns plateau; the resolution diagnostic warns otherwise).

    4. What this does and does not show¶

    • The fitters reproduce their own input curves to the printed residuals. That is a fit, not a validation.
    • A part is labelled validated only when Part.acceptance_check() reproduces the datasheet E_on / E_off (data held out of the fit) at the datasheet test condition within the part's declared tolerance (at most 25 %). No shipped part carries that label today: the shipped part files are synthetic illustrations and are refused by part number unless allow_synthetic=True.
    • The Level-1 channel has one constant K; a real part's transconductance rolls off at high current, which can move E_on by more than the tolerance (see docs/known-limitations.md).
