    Numerical Solvers¶

    Pulsim's transient kernel supports two fixed-step integrators (Tustin / BDF1) baked into pulsim.simulate(...), plus two adaptive Runge-Kutta integrators (DormandPrince5 and RadauIIA3) usable standalone against any user-supplied f(t, x) → dx/dt callback.

    Quick decision table¶

    Need Use
    Default SMPS / converter sim pulsim.simulate(...) (Tustin fixed-step)
    Stiff circuit (RC ≪ dt) pulsim.simulate(..., integrator="bdf1") (L-stable, fixed)
    Non-stiff ODE outside pulsim DormandPrince5(f=...).solve(...)
    Stiff ODE outside pulsim RadauIIA3(f=...).solve(...)
    Variable step inside pulsim shipped — engine='dsed' (event-driven RK45/BDF2 auto-dispatch) for LTI-per-mask circuits; in-kernel adaptive trap for the PWL engine still planned

    Fixed-step integrators (in-kernel)¶

    Tustin (trapezoidal companion) — default¶

    • 2nd-order, A-stable, NOT L-stable.
    • Cheap: 1 sparse solve per step.
    • Used for the bread-and-butter PWM-switched-mode workflow.
    • discontinuity_restart=True (off by default): the step containing a mask change, diode flip, source jump (or a b_extra_fn jump declared through next_jump_after(t)) or inconsistent start, and the next two, are Alexander SDIRK(2,2) steps (γ = 1 − 1/√2, L-stable). Each is two backward-Euler-like stages of γ·dt on one factor per mask at 2γ·dt. The result is order 2 across edges on the grid instead of 1, with no alternation after them, and resonant modes keep their energy. The gate is sampled at the step start, and a source reads its left limit at a jump on the step end. engine='trbdf2' and 'dsed' refuse it. With it, enable_substep_state_correction=True splits a step at an event inside it (a gate edge of a schedule with next_edge_after, a built-in source jump, a located diode crossing): trapezoid to the event, then the SDIRK window from it. Edges off the grid become second order (buck D = 0.4567, v_C at T/50: 8.08e-2 V snapped, 1.07e-6 V split); res.n_split_steps counts the split steps. split_snap (a fraction of dt, default 1e-6; 2.1.0's was 0.01) is the split's snap band: an event that close to a grid point is taken at it instead of cutting a sliver, and events that close merge. Pass split_snap=0.01 for 2.1.0's behaviour; it must lie in [1e-12, 0.25] and needs the split on (OptionsError otherwise). A jump the restart finds (or an ideal element's) is reported by res.reinit_records; an in-loop recorder's lines start from the post-jump state, so a window integral is the regular part, as on engine='dsed'.
    • Resolution diagnostics (on by default, read-only): the trapezoid's local error (h/12)|f_{k+1} − 2f_k + f_{k−1}| per linear C/L (f = i/C, v/L from the companion), over the variable's running peak, on three steps under one mask without restart, split, retry or source jump; plus a Nyquist-ringing detector (Δ_k·Δ_{k−1} < 0, |Δ_k| above 1 % of the local magnitude, four steps). Recorded in res.resolution; pulsim.ResolutionWarning above 1e-2, on a run or when over half the steps are unestimated. Opt-in: resolution_diagnostics=True.

    BDF1 (implicit Euler)¶

    • 1st-order, L-stable.
    • Used for stiff systems where Tustin would oscillate at large dt.
    • Enabled by opts.advanced.timestep.integrator = Bdf1.

    Newton on nonlinear devices (pwl and trbdf2)¶

    Circuits with Newton devices (Shockley diodes, MOSFET Level-1, saturable magnetics) solve J_lin + J_nl each iteration. Each run keeps one Newton workspace: the combined Jacobian is written in place on the union pattern of the two operands, and one sparse-LU analysis is reused for as long as that pattern holds (checked after every device refresh; a switch state with another pattern, or the static dt = 0 system, analyses again). Results are bit-identical to analysing every iteration (update-newton-persistent-solver, stage A1). Since stage A2 the factorisation is the in-house sparse LU, refactorised in place with no allocation per iteration; the DC operating point keeps Eigen::SparseLU. result.newton reports the run's counters (iterations, analyses, factorizations, …) and the iteration count of every Newton call (iterations_per_solve). The Levenberg-Marquardt fallback still factorises from scratch.

    Adaptive Runge-Kutta integrators (Phase 2.4, standalone)¶

    Both integrators ship as pure-Python classes in python/pulsim/integrators.py. They run against any f(t, x) → dx/dt callback (numpy array in, numpy array out), not yet coupled to the in-kernel pulsim.simulate solve — that requires reformulating the cache to expose continuous matrices, queued for v1.6.0.

    DormandPrince5 — RK 5(4)¶

    7-stage FSAL embedded pair (the "RK45" of MATLAB / SciPy fame).

    • 5th-order solution + 4th-order embedded error estimate.
    • FSAL → 6 effective f evals per step (vs 7).
    • PI step controller with safety factor 0.9, growth ≤ 5×.
    • Default rtol=1e-5, atol=1e-8.
    import pulsim as p
import numpy as np

def lorenz(t, x, sigma=10, rho=28, beta=8/3):
    return np.array([sigma*(x[1]-x[0]),
                       x[0]*(rho-x[2]) - x[1],
                       x[0]*x[1] - beta*x[2]])

solver = p.DormandPrince5(f=lorenz, rtol=1e-8, atol=1e-10)
res = solver.solve(t_span=(0.0, 10.0), x0=np.array([1.0, 1.0, 1.0]))
print(f"{res.n_accepted} accepted, {res.n_rejected} rejected, "
      f"{res.n_f_evals} f evals")

    RadauIIA3 — implicit, L-stable¶

    2-stage Radau IIA, order 3.

    • L-stable — handles stiff ODEs that crash RK45.
    • Newton inner solve on the 2n × 2n block system.
    • Step-doubling error estimate (Richardson) — robust + simple.
    • Optional analytical Jacobian via jac=callable; defaults to central finite differences.

    Speedup demo¶

    examples/scripts/run_adaptive_rk_speedup.py — van der Pol stiff oscillator (μ=100):

    Integrator Steps Wall-clock vs Euler
    Fixed-step Euler (dt=10 µs) 49,999 86.3 ms baseline
    DormandPrince5 (rtol=1e-4) 56 3.7 ms 23× faster
    RadauIIA3 (rtol=1e-4) 18 7.0 ms 12× faster

    DOPRI5 vs Radau final states agree to 1.49e-7. DOPRI5 wins wall-clock because each step is cheap (explicit). Radau wins step count because L-stability lets dt grow several orders of magnitude through the stiff transitions, at the cost of a Newton solve per step.

    Variable-step transient engine (engine='trbdf2')¶

    TR-BDF2 (γ = 2 − √2) with an embedded error estimate over capacitor voltages and inductor currents, gate edges landed exactly and diode crossings localised by Illinois on trial solves. Two restart rules after a gate edge:

    • Derivative refresh. The zero-time diode cascade at an edge solves a short trapezoidal probe (h_probe) under the post-edge mask; that probe is committed as a recorded step, so the history's inductor voltages, capacitor currents and stateful-device slots are post-edge values and the probe sample carries the post-edge switch state for loss post-processing. At an edge settled at a step boundary only the slots are replaced (the states are kept). The first post-edge step is then sized by a true error estimate. Each edge adds one recorded sample h_probe after it, so np.diff(res.times).min() is the probe length, not a controller step. Not done at diode events or at the initial settle (a diode turn-off leaves an inductor on a g_off-only path whose probe derivative is a picosecond mode).
    • Post-edge memory. Per edge parity (rising/falling), the step the controller proposes after the first accepted post-edge step, capped at h_max, is where the next edge of that parity restarts. It can grow as well as shrink. Diode events have no memory.

    Error control:

    • Estimate. The local error estimate carries the factor 2 of Bank et al. (1985), so it is the true local error to leading order; before, it was half of it and rtol meant twice itself. The Python defaults (rtol=1e-5, atol=1e-8) are unchanged; doubled values give the previous step density.
    • Ceiling and floors. The default ceiling is min(span/1000, period/20 of a periodic source, pulse width/3); dt overrides it. The step floor, probe floor, probe cap, chatter floor and gate-pulse floor are fixed fractions of 1000·h_max (exactly the old span fractions while the ceiling is span/1000), so with a ceiling set by dt or a source the steps up to a time do not depend on t_end. h_max_basis: 0 span, 1 source, 2 user.
    • Source breakpoints. Every corner of a pulse source (start, end of rise, start and end of fall, per period) and every edge of a PWM voltage source is landed like a gate edge; the landing step reads the source's left limit and the engine restarts as after a gate edge (n_source_breakpoints).

    res._trbdf2_stats reports n_derivative_refreshes, n_refresh_probes and h_post_event_min (smallest accepted first step after a refreshed edge).

    For developers: where the stepper lives¶

    Both transient engines (pwl, fixed step, in solver/run_transient.hpp; trbdf2, variable step, in solver/trbdf2_transient.hpp) run on one step driver. The shared pieces are in core/include/pulsim/solver/step/:

    header what it holds
    stepper.hpp step::run<I>(clock, body): the loop over the engine's phases (poll, attempt, accept or reject, commit, end)
    controllers.hpp FixedClock, LteController (step proposal, reject_factor) and ControllerState, the controller's part of a snapshot
    integrators.hpp stage tables: Trapezoidal, BackwardEuler, TrBdf2, Sdirk22 (incl. the restart estimate restart_gamma / lte / lte_q)
    esdirk43.hpp the Esdirk43 table
    policies.hpp HalvingLadder (fixed-step sub-step retry), GuardPolicy (inductor freeze, then clamp), RestartPolicy (the fixed engine's SDIRK restart steps)
    stepper_core.hpp commit, snapshot_into, restore, seed: accept, rollback and seeding over the history and the devices
    source_accumulator.hpp the source vector of a step
    sensitivity.hpp forward sensitivities along the stages

    What each engine still owns, and why (the decisions are in openspec/changes/refactor-unified-transient-stepper/DECISIONS.md):

    • Its solve point (DECISIONS 22). The fixed engine writes its one Newton call per segment out in the event iteration, because a closure on its step path cost more than the 0.5 % budget; TR-BDF2 keeps solve_stage / dispatch_solve.
    • The step decisions (DECISIONS 15, 25): reject, forced accept, the chatter guard, the event iteration (run_event_iteration) and the TR-BDF2 probe / landing phases stay in the engine's body. As free functions they would take the engine's whole state; they move when the event-driven loop owns that state (task groups 14 and 15).
    • The W⁻¹ restart filter (DECISIONS 24): two solves on the step's factor, so it stays with the other solves in run_transient_trbdf2.

    Changes here are behaviour-preserving by contract: results, counters included, are checked byte for byte against stored baselines (benchmarks/tools/pwl_identity.py, benchmarks/tools/trbdf2_identity.py).

    Tuning guide¶

    Knob DormandPrince5 RadauIIA3 Effect
    rtol 1e-5 1e-5 Larger → fewer steps, lower accuracy.
    atol 1e-8 1e-8 Floor on the error scale (for states near zero).
    dt_min 1e-12 1e-12 Raises if controller wants smaller (typically means tolerances too tight).
    dt_max ∞ ∞ Cap for non-stiff regions to avoid skipping events.
    safety 0.9 0.9 Multiplier on optimal step estimate.
    growth_max 5.0 5.0 Max single-step growth factor.
    newton_max_iter n/a 8 Radau: max Newton iterations per step.
    newton_tol n/a 1e-8 Radau: Newton convergence threshold.
    jac n/a None Radau: analytical Jacobian callable, else finite diff.

    What's NOT here (v1.5)¶

    • In-kernel coupling — replace Tustin / BDF1 inside pulsim.simulate with DormandPrince5 / RadauIIA3. Needs PwlStateSpaceCache to expose continuous A, b matrices (currently Tustin-discretized). Scope: 3-4 weeks kernel refactor. Queued for v1.6.0.
    • Dense-output interpolants — needed for precise switching-event localisation. Both algorithms support them (Hermite for DOPRI5, Lagrange for Radau); not exposed in this release.
    • DOPRI8(7) higher-order — only added on demand.
    • Rosenbrock / Rodas semi-implicit (no Newton, one linear solve) — DOPRI5 + Radau cover the common cases.

    The exact engine's output rule (output_rtol)¶

    With engine='exact', no dt, store_every or recorders, output_rtol chooses the stored samples: every event instant (both limits of a jump), and between events only the samples a tolerance needs. The promise: the straight line between two consecutive stored samples is within max(output_rtol * range, atol, floor) of the exact solution at every instant of the line, for every stored column (states, node voltages, branch currents). range is the column's range over the run so far (through the end of the exact step holding the line, at least 1e-3 of its peak); atol is the run's absolute tolerance (default 1e-9). No column is promised below its own rounding floor (floor, 16·eps times the sum of the magnitudes of the terms it is evaluated from): a column whose bound only fits through its floor is certified constant up to rounding at that floor and counted in res.diagnostics['output_grid'] (n_output_floored, output_floor_max, output_floor_by_column). A fast mode whose content does not decay between two instants of one flow as its own closed form says (the eigenbasis's error, not a transient) is taken out of the bound of a step it cuts; where that lengthens the step the bound with it is the column's floor there: counted in n_output_inconsistent, with one RuntimeWarning (output_inconsistent). A source corner (a pulse or PWM edge) is always a stored sample. The sample stored at an event is its left limit (the right limit is res.right_limit_states, pulsim.with_event_limits); after an event that changed the mode or a source's piece the solution 2 ps later is a stored sample as well, so plain interpolation of res.times draws a jump of a node voltage as a jump (over those 2 ps, the one line the promise does not cover) and not as a ramp to the next sample. The "exact solution" of the promise is the flow WITH the parasitic cluster projected at each event: modes whose time constant is below 1e-6 of the run's resolved time scale are settled at the event instant (the rule-off run lets them decay; a diode turn-off's ~1 ns mode read 0.31 V apart for a few ns). Every projection is recorded in diagnostics['output_grid']: n_projections, projection_max_by_column (per stored column the largest amplitude a projection moved it by) and projections (the first 1000: instant t, largest amplitude, its column, the cluster's slowest time constant tau). Recorders on such a run read a window's min / max inside each exact step from its closed form where one exists (a constant plus one damped sinusoid or at most two real exponentials); elsewhere the step ends are kept and counted (res.recorder_feed['closed_form']['n_extrema_ends']). Where the 4096-sample cap per interval, a mode without a diagonalising basis, or the run's 2e6-sample budget suspends the promise, the diagnostics say so and the run warns. benchmarks/tools/output_rule_referee.py checks the promise on the benchmark set.
