    Transmission line (Bergeron)¶

    b.add_transmission_line(name, a, a_ref, b, b_ref, Z0=..., delay=..., model="bergeron") adds a lossless line of surge impedance Z0 and one-way travel time delay (τ). Each port follows the travelling-wave law

    v − Z0·i = E(t),   E_a(t) = w_b(t − τ),   E_b(t) = w_a(t − τ),

    where w = v + Z0·i is the wave a port sends and i is the current into the line. The line has no lumped approximation and no section count. A front leaves one end and arrives at the other exactly τ later. The older model="pi" (a lumped ladder that overshoots a step by 27 %) is still the default.

    R= adds a series resistance as R/4, two half-lines, R/2 and R/4 (sub-elements {name}.ra, {name}.rm, {name}.rb). The loss sits in those resistors. R > Z0 is refused. A lossless line stores energy in flight and dissipates none.

    Worked example: the lattice diagram¶

    A 1 V step behind 10 Ω drives a 50 Ω, 100 ns line with an open far end. The incident wave is V+ = 50/60 V. The source reflects with Γs = (10 − 50)/60 = −2/3 and the open end with ΓL = +1. After (2n+1)·τ the far end therefore steps to 2·V+·Σ_{j≤n} Γs^j, that is 5/3 V, 5/9 V and 35/27 V.

    import pulsim as p
b = p.CircuitBuilder()
b.add_pulse_voltage_source("V", "s", "gnd", 0.0, 1.0, 10e-9, 1.0, 0.0, 1e-12, 1e-12)
b.add_resistor("Rs", "s", "k", 10.0)
b.add_transmission_line("T", "k", "gnd", "m", "gnd", Z0=50.0, delay=100e-9,
                        model="bergeron")
r = p.simulate(b, t_end=700e-9, dt=1e-9, engine="pwl")

    The far-end plateaus come out as 1.666666666667, 0.555555555556 and 1.296296296296 V, relative error ≤ 2.2e-16. The script is examples/scripts/run_transmission_line.py. It also drives a quarter-wave transformer: at f = 1/(4τ) a 10 Ω load appears as Z0²/R_L = 250 Ω.

    Engines¶

    Engine Line
    pwl (fixed step) Yes. dt > τ is refused by name (the far end would be in the future). With τ an integer multiple of dt the far end reads a stored sample: exact to rounding. Otherwise it is interpolated linearly: second order in dt, never amplifying, with fronts smeared over one dt and plateaus exact.
    trbdf2 Yes. The step is capped at τ (_trbdf2_stats["n_line_bound_steps"] counts the steps the line set). A step lands where a wave the run resolved at one end arrives at the other: at a source corner + τ, and at t_k + τ for every recorded sample where a wave changes slope by more than h_max could interpolate within atol + rtol·|w|. Fronts, corners and reflections arrive on a step boundary, and the effect stops once the waves are smooth to the tolerance. On a matched line the rejects equal those of the same circuit without the line.
    trbdf2, integrator="esdirk43" Refused by name: linear interpolation of the line's past would cut its order to 2 and make its error estimate lie.
    exact / dsed, bdf1 Refused by name (the capability table).
    AC sweep Yes, exact: the line's two-port (ABCD) rows at each frequency, θ = ωτ. A quarter wave turns R_L into Z0²/R_L.
    DC operating point A wire (v_b = v_a, i_a = −i_b). A transient from start_from_dc_op=True starts with the DC waves, so it starts without a wave.
    periodic_steady_state Refused by name: the line's state is its last τ of waves, which is not in the node vector.

    What the error control does not see¶

    On trbdf2 the step is chosen by the circuit's reactive states, capped at τ, plus the landings above. The line's interpolation error is not part of the error estimate, and a wave is only as fine as the steps that first recorded it. On a circuit whose only dynamics is the line (resistors and lines) driven by a smooth source, nothing refines the step below τ, whatever rtol is. A 3.3 MHz sine through a 100 ns line is then off by 6.6 mV of 0.5 V at any rtol. Set the step ceiling (dt= on trbdf2) to resolve the waves you care about, or use pwl with τ a multiple of dt.

    Results and snapshots¶

    res.i("T") is the current into port a and res.i("T.b") the current into port b. A resume (resume_from=) restores the line's recorded past (snapshot field line_state), so a resumed run equals the uninterrupted one.
